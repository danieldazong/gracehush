## Pastoral Tone Rules (step 32)

How a reflection sounds. The instruction is `process-entry/prompt.ts` (`PROMPT_VERSION` `gen-v2.7`, built on 2026-11-23 and live from its deploy: plain speech, the way a caring friend talks, and words that fit any hour of the day; `gen-v2.6` on 2026-11-10, the closing prayer in five moves that meet the need beneath the person's words; `gen-v2.5` on 2026-11-08, step 37, a Bible verse under each affirmation; `gen-v2.4` the same day, the heavy moments; `gen-v2.3` on 2026-11-07, step 36), the checks code can make are `process-entry/tone.ts` and `output.ts`, and the evaluation is `process-entry/eval/`. The crisis screening (Crisis Screening Rules) is not part of this and does not change with it.

- **Written from the entry (step 36).** On 2026-11-07 three customers reported reflections that were generic, repetitive or "nothing to do with what I said". Since gen-v2.3, every reflection is written from the person's own entry: the first line on the screen (the validation line), the devotional, the closing prayer and the affirmation texts use what they told, in their words, and add nothing they did not say. Code checks what it can (the prayer's voice, grounding, the line, the echo, below), and the evaluation's swap test measures whether a judge can tell which entry a reflection was written for.
- **A heavy moment (gen-v2.4, 2026-11-08, the owner's decision).** An entry the screening or the model flags (Crisis Safety Rules) is written in full, by every rule here and by the section "A heavy moment" (`HEAVY_INSTRUCTION` in `process-entry/heavy.ts`, at the end of the instruction), whose rules come first where they meet another. When the screening found it, the user turn ends with a `<heavy_moment>` block (`buildUserTurn(…, heavy)`; a `<heavy_moment>` tag the person typed is removed like the other trusted tags). It stays with the pain and declares who God is to them now, never repeats or describes what they wrote about dying or harm, never says it in softer words or denies it, never says the wish makes sense, never presents death as rest or a way home, never calls their feelings sin, weakness or a test, never tells someone who is hurt to forgive, obey or stay, promises no quick end, quotes nothing from the entry, and its next step is always a person to reach out to today. The passage comforts and steadies, never one of `DENIED_PASSAGES` (a denied pick is replaced, and Isaiah 41:10, `HEAVY_FALLBACK_PASSAGE`, is the last resort). Hard checks for a heavy reply: a quotation in the devotional or the prayer (`heavy_quote`) and the phrases of `HEAVY_NEVER_SAY` (`dying`, `self_harm`, `wish`, `method`, none negatable) in anything the person reads or hears; grounding and the echo are not checked for it (they would pull the person's words back in). When no reply passes, the handler saves `heavyFallback()`, which `heavy_test.ts` holds to every check. The live check has three heavy cases, and a one-off run of eight made-up heavy entries on 2026-11-08 gave eight whole reflections with no never-say phrase, no quotation and no denied passage (it found a quiet case that said "never wake up" and that the wish "makes human sense", which is why `wish` and the two instruction lines about it exist).
- **The order of care.** Open inside their moment with the hook (below), then name what the person seems to feel, in plain words, and honor it as real; stay with it for a few sentences before any hope (never a "but" right after naming it); then tell them one true thing about who God is to them (that he is with them, that he cares, that he keeps his promises, that Jesus knows what hurting feels like), simply and surely, not a fix; end with one small, gentle next step that grows out of what they told (a sentence to pray, one thing to hand over to God), never a breathing or body exercise and never a list of tasks, with a last line that is short and warm, never a slogan (gen-v2.7). Step 36 took "a slow breath" out of the examples: in the first evaluation round it ended 27 to 31 of 32 devotionals (31 of 32 under gen-v2.2 too), a closing template the grader read as coaching. An entry that is mostly joy or gratitude is met with gladness first, and then God is thanked with the person.
- **Plain speech (gen-v2.7, 2026-11-23; it replaced the sermon voice of 2026-10-31).** The project lead heard build 11 and found the voice natural and the words a sermon ("so structured, so organized … a human being does not talk like that"); the owner asked for reflections "as human as possible, like a real human that a 9 years old can understand". Everything a reflection says or shows (the validation line, the devotional, the closing prayer, the affirmation texts) is written the way a caring Christian friend talks across the kitchen table: never a pastor preaching, a writer making beautiful sentences, a book or a greeting card. A nine-year-old understands every sentence the first time they hear it, and an adult who is tired or hurting is never talked down to: plain, never childish, no baby talk, no exclamation marks. Most sentences are under 15 words, now and then a longer simple one joined with "and", never over 25. A contraction every time a person would say one ("God hasn’t left you", never "God has not left you"), and never "do not", "does not", "is not", "cannot" or "will not" in the devotional or the prayer. Everyday words, with the instruction's swap list (look, not gaze; tired, not weary; tiredness, not weariness; in the middle of, not amid; keeps his promises, not faithfulness; a safe place, not a refuge; these days, not this season); no word picture that needs explaining, no "not this, but that", no lists of three, no line built to sound wise, no sentence that sounds like a saying; people and God do the things in a sentence, never ideas or feelings; God's truth said plainly and surely, without hedging, and never as a command to the person or a promised outcome; nothing about the writer itself ("I'm sorry", "I know"). The instruction's examples come from no evaluation case (a test holds that), and neither the lead's example nor any whole sample paragraph goes into it (a template the model copies). The devotional and the prayer still never quote, paraphrase, cite or point to the passage; sharing an everyday word with it ("near", "help", "rest") is fine, its sentences and phrases are not (gen-v2.6's ban on its key words drove rarer synonyms), and they tell a different true thing about God than the passage tells, so that the passage, read right after, adds something new (a plain restatement of it is still a paraphrase). The prayer keeps its five moves in plain words (below).
- **The plain-speech check (gen-v2.7).** `plainSpeechProblem` in `tone.ts` fires on a word of `PLAIN_AVOID_WORDS` (words a friend never needs, each form listed on its own) in the validation line, the devotional or the prayer, or on a devotional whose reading grade (`readingGrade`, Flesch-Kincaid with `syllables`, a consistent rule of thumb) is over `PLAIN_RETRY_GRADE` (7). A word the entry itself uses and the quoted echo are set aside. The affirmation texts are measured, never checked (they take words from their verse). It is a soft check: one retry with a note that names only the list's own words (at most eight), and a reply no better is kept as written, `salvaged:plain_speech`. A word joins `PLAIN_AVOID_WORDS` only when a friend never needs it, it is not a word of the faith (God, Lord, Jesus, grace, mercy, faith, faithful, hope, love, peace, prayer…), and no fixed text holds it (the written fallback, the safe title and headlines, the line's example); `PLAINER_WORDS` (burden, faithful, mercy, tender, weary…) are measured only. The rules of both checks are exact: the evaluation's bars were measured with them, so they are never "improved" in place. Faith, the never-say list, "The person", the heavy section and the written fallback (but its affirmations, below) are unchanged, word for word (a test compares them with frozen gen-v2.6).
- **Any hour of the day (gen-v2.7, the owner's report of 2026-11-23).** Nothing the model writes names a part of the day or guesses when it will be heard or read (no "tonight", "this morning", "before you sleep", "when you wake", "as you start your day"): a devotional is often replayed hours later, the Word Deck sends any word at any reminder, the Today card keeps a word until the next reminder, and Reflection shows the three affirmations together. "Today" and "right now" stay, and so does a time the person told ("you couldn't sleep last night"). The instruction has a section of its own, "Any hour of the day", right after "Written to be read aloud". `timeOfDayProblem` in `tone.ts` fires on a word of `TIME_WORDS` (parts of the day, sleep and waking) or a phrase of `TIME_PHRASES` ("start your day", "end of the day", "lay your head") in the validation line, the devotional, the prayer, a headline or an affirmation text, wider than the plain check because the headlines are what other hours see; the entry's own words and phrases and the echo are set aside. It works like the plain check: one retry with a note that names only the list's words or the phrase the model wrote, then the reply as written, `salvaged:time_of_day`. The owner chose this over words timed by the phone's clock, which would still be wrong on a replay and needs an app build; the time of day lives only where the app reads the clock as it shows something (the greeting, the reminder times the person chose).
- **Each devotional sounds new (2026-10-31).** The spoken script starts with the devotional itself. There is no fixed line ("Here is your devotional." went because, with it and a model that began 20 of 24 devotionals with "It", every devotional sounded the same) and no title (gen-v2.1 read the title first, which delayed the hook and repeated across one person's devotionals: three titles in a row held "Waiting"). The devotional never begins with a stock opening (`STOCK_OPENINGS` in `tone.ts`, which the instruction lists word for word: "It", "I", "The", "Today", "Sometimes", "Dear", "You are not alone", "You’re not alone", "In this moment", "Let’s"; gen-v2.2 forbids every "The", which had begun hooks that sounded alike, such as "The phone…" and "The screen…"). When recent reflections are given it begins with a different first word and kind of hook from each, and its title takes main words of its own. gen-v2.3 has no tone example (step 36, decision 6): one example in every prompt is a template the model copies. `TONE_EXAMPLE` stays in `tone.ts` for the evaluation's copied-example measure and the tests.
- **The first line is a hook (gen-v2.2, the user's requirement of 2026-10-31).** The devotional's first sentence is the first thing heard and must capture the person within the first five seconds, so that even after a hundred devotionals each one feels written for them alone. It is built from one concrete thing they told (something they did, saw, heard or keep doing, the moment it happened, or their exact situation), in the model's own words or in theirs (gen-v2.3), and said to them, with "you" or "your" in it, the way a friend starts talking, never a written scene or an idea doing what a person does, beginning with the moment, the time it happened or something they did and only now and then with "you" (gen-v2.7), with their facts kept exactly (nothing added or changed: a place, an object or a gesture they did not mention is a made-up detail), as one full sentence with its own verb (not an -ing phrase); it is at most 12 words (`HOOK_MAX_WORDS`), lets the concrete thing carry the feeling (no feeling word such as "heavy", "weight" or "burden" in it: the evaluation counts them, `HOOK_FEELING_WORDS`), speaks straight into their moment (no general truth, poetic abstraction or scene-setting) and could never open another entry's devotional. Since gen-v2.3 it may name what they told (the interview, the car, the surgery), but never a person's name: it calls them what the person called them ("your son", "your friend"). **The hook guard** (`hookProblem` in `tone.ts`, last in `parseReflection`) is the one style rule that may cost a retry, because the user asked for it: a devotional that begins with a stock opening (`hook_stock`), with the first word one of the person's recent devotionals began with (`hook_repeated`; the handler passes those first words), or with a first sentence over 16 words (`hook_long`, `HOOK_RETRY_WORDS`) is asked for once more with a note that names only stock words and the model's own first words. When no better reply comes the reply is kept as it was, so a reflection never fails over its opening, and the 200 line's `reason` is `salvaged:hook_stock`, `salvaged:hook_repeated` or `salvaged:hook_long`. Known limit: the model sometimes changes a small fact in the hook (a porch for a driveway); the instruction and the grader ask against it, and code cannot check it.
- **Faith.** The historic Christian faith that Christians share across traditions, Christ-centered and grace-first. No side on questions that divide churches (baptism, the end times, spiritual gifts, women in ministry, predestination). No promised outcome (healing, a job, a restored relationship): God's presence whatever happens. Never that suffering is a punishment, or that more faith would have prevented it. Doubt, anger at God and unanswered prayer are honest prayers, met without correcting the person. A trusted friend, pastor or counselor may be gently suggested; never a diagnosis, and never advice about medication or treatment.
- **Never said, or anything that means the same:** everything happens for a reason; God won’t give you more than you can handle; just pray, just trust God or just have faith; "at least" comparisons that make the pain smaller; any reason why someone died; commands such as "you should" or "you need to"; promises that everything will work out.
- **The person** is spoken to as "you", with nothing assumed about gender, marriage, children, church life or age. In the devotional and the closing prayer, what happened, where and what they face is named in their own plain words (the surgery on Thursday, the interview, the rent, the car), and other people by the role or the name the person used ("my son", "my manager"). Nothing is added: never a name, a place, an illness or a detail they did not give, and never a guess (step 36, decision 2, which superseded gen-v2.2's "never repeat a name, a place, an illness or a medical detail" for these two texts). The title, the validation line, the affirmation headlines and the prayer summary keep that rule: a stranger can read them, so they never hold a name, and the title and the headlines never say what happened.
- **One echo.** Once in the devotional the model may quote a short phrase of the person's exactly as they said it, at most eight words, in quotation marks, woven into a sentence of its own, and only when their words say it better. It is never given a set framing: the prompt's example "You said it yourself: …" was copied into 16 to 19 of 32 devotionals in the first evaluation round, a template like the tone example, so the instruction now names it as one never to use. Code checks every quotation in the devotional and the prayer together: one span of at most eight words that is a run of the entry's words (case, punctuation and the apostrophe's shape aside), or none at all (`echoProblem`, `echo_invented`).
- **The prayer is the person's own, and it meets the need beneath their words (gen-v2.3, decision 1; gen-v2.6, 2026-11-10).** The closing prayer is in the first person singular ("I", "my", "me"), addressed as they addressed God in the entry ("Father", "Lord", "Jesus", "God", else "Lord"). "We", "us" and "our" only when the person themselves prayed as "we"; a prayer for someone else is still "I" ("help me be patient with her", never "help us"). The spoken intro stays "Let's pray.". Since gen-v2.6 (the owner's report of 2026-11-10: the reflection for an entry that said "I failed my exam and I want to drop out" prayed "help me as I just want to drop out and walk away", which met nothing and lifted nothing, where gen-v2.3's rule had asked for "each thing they asked for, in their words") it moves in five steps, in this order, and since gen-v2.7 in the plain words of "Voice", the way a person really prays when they're tired or worried, with short sentences: (1) it talks to God the way the person talked to him in the entry, simply, as to someone right there (gen-v2.6 named him "by who he is for this need", which gave "Lord, the God who guides every step"; gen-v2.7 dropped it); (2) it hands him the pain once, in the person's own plain words and with no quotation marks; (3) it asks for two or three concrete things that meet the need beneath their words, each in a short sentence of its own that opens with the asking ("Help me", "Give me", "Show me", "Please"; strength for today, a clear head for the choice ahead, real rest, courage to make the call, the will to try again, enough for the rent; never the old church "Grant me"), one of them tied to the next step the devotional offered, and every petition stays "me" and "my" also for another person or a shared home ("give me the right words for my wife", "help me find what my home needs", never "us" or "between us" unless the person wrote "we" or "our"); it prays for the need and never for a despairing wish itself: when the person says they want to drop out, give up, quit, walk away or disappear, it neither asks God to let them nor argues with them, and asks instead for rest and clear sight before any decision and for his wisdom in it; (4) it tells him one true thing about who he is to them now, in simple words of its own, with no promised outcome (that he is with them in this, that he is already at work, that his care does not depend on how today went; never that the exam, the job, the test or the person will turn out well); (5) it ends with one line of trust in who God is. 5 to 7 sentences, 50 to 75 words and never more (2 to 4 sentences and 30 to 50 words until gen-v2.6: about 30 words more, which is about 10 seconds more of audio at 14 characters a second, not the "about 2 seconds" first said to the owner). The rule is the exported constant `CLOSING_PRAYER_RULE` in `prompt.ts`, which the tests pin. Code checks the voice as before (`prayerVoice`: `i` with at least two singular pronouns and no plural one, or plural ones only where the entry holds one; `we` with a plural the entry does not explain; `none` otherwise; only `i` passes, and `we` when the entry holds a plural pronoun: `prayer_voice:we`, `prayer_voice:none`), and since gen-v2.6 measures the petitions (`prayerPetitions` in `tone.ts`: the clauses that open with a verb of asking once the address is set aside, or that say "I ask", "I pray" or "please"; a style measure, never a refusal) and the length (`prayerInRange`, 50 to 75; `prayerSentences`); the grader's `prayer_meets_need` judges the rest. Known limits: a prayer for "my sister and me" that says "help us both" counts as `we`; the petition count is a heuristic (a clause that opens with "clear sight before I decide" is not counted, and two petitions joined by "and" count as two only when the second opens with its own verb); the heavy fallback's written prayer (`heavy.ts`, 44 words in three sentences) keeps the gen-v2.5 shape, because its words are the owner's to approve.
- **The validation line (gen-v2.3, decision 4).** One sentence of 8 to 18 words in the second person, with "you" or "your" in it, that names what the person is going through and what they seem to feel, in plain words from their entry, and honors it as real ("Your son's surgery is Thursday and your hands won't stop shaking, and that's real."). It is the first line on the Reflection screen, above the devotional, and it is not read aloud. It never begins with a stock opening, "It sounds like", "It feels like" or "It seems", and holds no fix, verse, promise or name of a person or a place. Two different entries must never get the same line. The schema field `validation_line` comes right after `reflection_title`, and a heavy moment has one too since gen-v2.4. Code checks it, in this order (`validationProblem`): `validation_missing`; `validation_length` (under 5 or over 24 words, or over 240 characters); `validation_stock`; `validation_person` (no "you" or "your"); and `validation_private` (a word the person capitalized, as for the title).
- **Grounding (gen-v2.3, decision 9).** The validation line, the devotional and the prayer together must share the entry's content stems: at least 2 when the entry has 8 or more, at least 1 with 3 to 7, and no check below 3 ("Thank you God for today"). A content word has at least four letters, is lowercased and possessive-cut, and is in neither `STOPWORDS` (about 150 common words) nor `DEVOTIONAL_WORDS` (the words any devotional holds: god, heart, peace, today, feel, carry, rest…). A stem cuts a plural, -ed or -ing when four letters remain, makes -ies and -ied into -y, and cuts -es only after a sibilant (`groundingCount`, `grounding_low`). Every 200 line carries the count.
- **The affirmation texts take three sides of what the person shared (gen-v2.7; decision 8 of step 36 named three hours).** In the plain words of "Voice": one about what they face, one about the worry they named, one about what they are carrying, resting on what God does rather than on self-belief, and none names a part of the day, so each fits any hour. The `time_slot` (morning, afternoon, evening) only says where an affirmation is sent first, and it is never the affirmation's subject. No name and no place. The headline keeps every rule it had (since step 37 it is a notification's bold title, over its verse).
- **A verse under each affirmation (gen-v2.5, step 37).** Each affirmation carries ONE verse of the Berean Standard Bible, chosen first and for what the person shared (never for the hour: never a verse about the morning, the night or sleep unless the person's words are about it, because the app may show it at any hour), never a wish or a blessing spoken over someone ("May the Lord…"), never a promise kept only for those who do something, never the speaker's plea (gen-v2.7), and its headline is written from that verse, never naming a part of the day and never its verse's own phrases, not even said more simply: 2 to 6 words, at most 38 characters (one bold line), spoken with "you" or "your", a truth and never an instruction, in fresh words and never the verse's own phrases, never one of the generic lines the instruction names ("You are held", "You are loved", …), and the three headlines begin with three different words; it speaks of what God is or does for them and never of their own state, body or reaction (never their tears, weakness, tiredness, failure or sleep), so a stranger learns nothing about them from it. The model says where the verse is (`verse` in each affirmation, before the headline in the schema); `process-entry/verses.ts` (`resolveAffirmationVerses`) reads its words from the Bible and keeps a verse only when it is one verse, at most 140 characters read as one line (`AFFIRMATION_VERSE_MAX_CHARS`), a whole sentence (a capital first, `.`, `!` or `?` last, quotes aside), about God (it holds a name of God or a pronoun the BSB capitalizes for him: `speaksOfGod`, a floor that refuses a verse about a person, such as Philemon 1:7), with no order in it (no sentence of it opens with an imperative: `givesAnOrder`, a floor that refuses "Give thanks…", "Bear with one another…" and the "Do not be afraid" at the end of Deuteronomy 31:8; the instruction asks for a declaration with no command anywhere and never the speaker's own resolve), not inside the reflection's passage, not another affirmation's, not one of the person's recent passages or affirmation verses, and, for a heavy moment, not one `DENIED_PASSAGES` names. Every verse that breaks a rule goes into ONE small follow-up request (`buildVersesInstruction`, `buildVersesTurn`: the headlines, the reasons, the references to avoid, the title and the feeling, never the person's words; one attempt, 4,096 output tokens, because thinking counts in the cap and 1,024 cut two replies off), which gives two verses per affirmation, best first (`VERSE_CANDIDATES`), each meeting the same rules, the second read only when the first breaks one; a verse still unusable leaves its affirmation without one, and a chapter that cannot be read leaves it without one with no follow-up. An affirmation the reply gave no verse costs no follow-up, so the written fallback (which names none) asks nothing. Two soft checks on the headlines (`headlineProblems` in `tone.ts`, after the private-word check): a feeling word, or since round 2 a word for the person's state (tears, crying, depleted, drained, weakness, failing and the like), in any headline (`headline_feeling`, `HEADLINE_FEELING_WORDS`) and a headline this person already received (`headline_repeated`, against `listRecentAffirmations`), each asked for once more with a note that quotes only the model's own headline, and kept as its salvage (`salvaged:headline_feeling`, `salvaged:headline_repeated`). The code capitalizes a headline's first letter. The evaluation grades the verses (`verse_fit`) and measures them (found, all three, from the follow-up) and the headlines (over 38 characters, a feeling word, generic, three that begin alike).
- **One more affirmation (add-v1, 2026-11-09).** `add-affirmation` asks for ONE affirmation that says something none of the reflection's affirmations says, by the verse and headline rules above word for word (`VERSE_RULE`, `HEADLINE_RULE` in `add-affirmation/prompt.ts`), with a text of 12 to 30 words tied to the person's day and to no hour, and one answer more: the lock-screen slot the new affirmation should take over, or "none", the usual answer, given only when it fits that hour of the person's day clearly better than the affirmation carrying it. The user turn holds the entry, the reflection's title, feeling and passage, and the affirmations the reflection already has (the slotted three first, in the slots' order, each as its headline and verse reference and the time of day it carries; never a text), and the heavy block for a heavy moment, whose "A heavy moment" section is the same as process-entry's. The faith, never-say, person and voice rules are process-entry's, written for one affirmation. **Measured on 2026-11-09** (`gemini-3.8-flash`, the fallback `gemini-3.1-flash-lite`; eight made-up entries with three made-up affirmations each, a heavy one among them, and seven additions in a row for each, through the function's own code with no database, about $0.17): 56 of 56 made; 55 with a verse (one affirmation left without); no headline over 38 characters (37 at most), none with a feeling word, none repeated within a reflection, 54 distinct of 56 across all eight (one headline came up in three different people's reflections, with Psalm 138:8 four times across them, which the recent-affirmations rule covers for one person and the evaluation did not pass in); no verse repeated within a reflection, 45 distinct of 55 across the eight; the slot answer "none" 56 times (the move is the rare case, as the instruction asks); no salvage; 20 of 56 needed a second model call (a correction, or the verse follow-up), median 3.5 s and 16.5 s at most; the heavy case's seven stayed on God's nearness and care (Psalm 73:23, 34:18, 139:14, 138:8, 121:5, 27:1, 149:4), with no denied passage. "The" began 13 of the 56 headlines: the instruction forbids only a first word one of the listed headlines begins with, and the recent-headlines rule in the function adds the person's thirty newest.
- **God's pronouns are lowercase** (he, him, his, you, your), as the Berean Standard Bible writes them; God, Lord, Father, Jesus, Christ and Holy Spirit are capitalized.
- **No scripture in the devotional or the closing prayer.** They never quote, paraphrase, cite or point to the passage or any other verse: the app shows and reads the passage on its own.
- **Written for the voice** (Chirp 3 HD reads it: a period is a full stop, a comma a short pause). Short, plain sentences as "Voice" asks, most under 15 words and never over 25, with a contraction every time a person would say one (gen-v2.7); no parentheses, slashes, semicolons, numerals, abbreviations, emojis or lists, and no quotation marks but around the one echo; at most one question; three or four short paragraphs; curly apostrophes.
- **Lengths.** The devotional is 110 to 150 words (about 130) and the closing prayer 5 to 7 sentences of 50 to 75 words since gen-v2.6 (2 to 4 sentences of 30 to 50 words before), which with the passage and the spoken framing is about 1,300 characters of script, about 93 seconds in Aoede at 0.88 (about 1,170 characters and 84 seconds under gen-v2.5, measured on the same 36 made-up cases on 2026-11-10; `SCRIPT_CHARS_PER_SECOND` is 14, measured in steps 22 and 26). The title is Title Case, 2 to 6 words, aiming at 40 characters, with no end punctuation; the headline is sentence case and second person, 2 to 6 words and at most 38 characters since step 37 (one bold line; the hard limit stays 2 to 8 words and 50 characters, `LIMITS.affirmationHeadline`); the affirmation's text is second person, 12 to 30 words; the validation line 8 to 18 words (the check allows 5 to 24 and 240 characters, `LIMITS.validationLine`); the prayer title stays sentence case. The hard limits in `LIMITS` are wider (a devotional of 80 to 220 words): they only stop a runaway reply.
- **The title and the headlines are lock-screen text.** The title is the lock-screen card's title while a devotional plays (step 26) and a headline is a notification's whole body (step 28), so both are checked against the person's own names: the words they capitalized in the middle of a sentence (`privateWords`, less the words of faith, the pronouns, the days, the months, a title before a name and the common short words). A headline never holds the feeling from the entry either, even when the affirmation's text below it names that feeling (the instruction asks it and lists sorrow, grief, worry, fear, guilt, sadness, exhaustion and loneliness; the title may be gently true to the feeling). Code does not check it; the grader's `lock_screen_safe` does. The validation line is checked against the same words (`validation_private`, step 36): it sits above the devotional, where anyone near the phone can read it. Known limit: a name written only at the start of a sentence, or in lowercase, is not found by the check (the instruction still forbids it).
- **Hard checks and style measures.** A hard check makes a reply unusable, so it is asked for again, and two unusable replies fail the entry with `AI_UNAVAILABLE` (the app retries it): the shape and limits, a verse reference in the devotional or the prayer, a never-say phrase (precise patterns, never single words; a sentence that denies one, such as "This is not a punishment for anything you did", passes) and a private word in the title or a headline. Everything else is a style measure (`styleMeasures`: long sentences, questions, symbols, numerals, "you should" and "at least", a capitalized pronoun for God, a copied tone example, a stock opening and the first word, the word targets), which never costs a reply: the evaluation counts it and the wording fixes it. Only what protects privacy, scripture or the person may become a hard check. The soft checks may cost a retry, never a reply or a reflection: the hook guard (above), since gen-v2.3 the prayer's voice, grounding, the validation line and the echo (decision 9), and since gen-v2.7 plain speech and the time of day (`plainSpeechProblem`, `timeOfDayProblem`). A reply whose only faults are soft is kept as a salvage when no clean reply comes, and the hard checks (a verse reference or a never-say phrase) cover the validation line too.
- **A retry is told what to fix.** After a tone failure the next attempt is the same request with a `<correction>` block at the end of the user turn (`withCorrection` in `gemini.ts`, on the main and the fallback model), naming the rule and, for a private word, the words to avoid (the entry's private words, the leaked ones first, at most 20). The note can hold the person's own words: it goes only into the retry of that same request (paid credit, `store: false`) and is never logged, stored or printed. The notes of step 36's checks name a rule and never a word of the person's (a test keeps it so), and gen-v2.7's two notes name only words of the code's lists or a phrase the model wrote, never the person's (tests keep it so). Several soft faults at once go in one retry with every note, and the first fault names the salvage. A reply that fails a shape check is asked for again with no note.
