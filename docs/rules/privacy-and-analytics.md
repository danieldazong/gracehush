# AI permission and analytics

> Part of the GraceHush rules, moved out of `AGENTS.md` word for word on 2026-11-23. `AGENTS.md` is the short core that every session loads, and its "Rules index" says which file holds which section. The sections below keep their original `##` headings, so a reference such as "AGENTS.md, Crisis Safety Rules" finds them. A rule that changes is changed here, in its section, in a few lines; a step's history goes to `docs/build-log.md`. This file can be long: search it (`git grep -n "<words>" docs/rules/privacy-and-analytics.md`) and read only the part you need.

## AI Permission Rules (2026-11-03; a sheet since 2026-11-07)

Apple's guideline 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so." A prayer goes to Google's Gemini and a devotional to Google Cloud Text-to-Speech, so nothing a person says or writes leaves the phone until their account has said yes. The wording was shown to the user first and approved on 2026-11-03, because it touches crisis handling (Crisis Safety Rules).

- **Why a sheet (2026-11-07).** The first version was a full page of small print with three rows. The owner met it in the first TestFlight build and said many people will not go on: it reads like a legal notice, and a person about to use their voice thinks it is about to train an AI. Apple's rule needs three things to be clear before anything is sent: what is sent, who gets it, and the person's permission. Two sentences say the first two and a button asks the third. The training answer stays on the face too, because Apple's Human Interface Guidelines for generative AI ask apps to ask permission and to say whether personal information is used for training, and because it is the very worry the page raised. Everything else (why it is asked, what Google keeps, where to change it) is one tap away. A system alert was considered and not chosen: Apple's guidelines keep alerts for critical information, to be used sparingly, and an alert cannot carry the Learn more link. The sheet is drawn inside the New Entry screen, not as a system Modal and not as a `BottomSheet` (Bottom Sheets, the exception). The cost is no swipe-down to dismiss; the scrim, the Android back key, VoiceOver's two-finger scrub and Not now are the ways out.
- **Where it is asked.** New Entry, every way into it (the dock's mic and pen, the empty state's button, "Edit and resend"): until the account has answered, `AiPermissionSheet` covers the screen. The recorder and the field are drawn behind it, out of reach of touch and of screen readers, and the keyboard waits for the answer, so nothing typed or recorded waits on it and "Not now" has nothing to lose. It is not asked in onboarding: the person has nothing to share yet, and the moment their words would first leave the phone is the moment the question means something.
- **What it says** is `copy.aiPermission`. The face holds the question, who receives what (recordings and words to Google's Gemini AI, and what it writes to Google's text-to-speech: since step 38 the words name no devotional, so they cover Scholar too), the answer to the training worry ("We don’t train AI on them, and Google’s paid terms say it doesn’t use them to improve its products.") and the promise that nothing is sent until Allow. It is at most 55 words, and a check keeps it so and keeps the whole sheet free of words that promise more than the app can ("secure", "encrypted", "guarantee", "safe", "free"). Under Learn more: why it is asked (to write the devotional and read it aloud, with extra care when the words carry something very heavy), what Google keeps (the words, for a limited time, to check for misuse) and where to change it (Profile), with the Privacy Policy link. The training answer is the one the Privacy Policy and the Terms give, and a check compares them. Google's terms and its Text-to-Speech page were read on 2026-11-03: read them again before changing the trust sentence, which speaks of Google's paid terms for the Gemini API and Cloud Text-to-Speech together, and a lawyer reads it before launch.
- **The one gate is the sync engine.** The outbox is the only way a person's words reach `process-entry` (only the engine calls `processItem`, and a check keeps it so), and `drain` takes nothing from it unless `hasAiPermission()` (`services/aiPermission.ts`) says yes; `arm` leaves the outbox's times out while it says no, or a held reflection would wake the engine every second. Prayer changes still go: they go to Supabase and to no AI. So does everything else that sends no words to a model: deleting a reflection, the export, `confirm-scripture`. Nobody signed in means no. No Edge Function, migration or secret changed.
- **The answer** is `aiPermissionAt` in the profile prefs store: when this account said yes, or null. It is per account and on this phone only (a second account is asked, a new phone or a reinstall asks again, and there is no server record), a sign-out keeps it and account deletion forgets it, and a damaged or missing saved value is no. `useAiPermission()` reads it for the screens; allowing wakes the engine (`syncNow("permission")`), so what was held goes at once.
- **"Not now" and every other way out** (the scrim, the Android back key, VoiceOver's two-finger scrub) return to Journal (`popTo`) with the note "Nothing was sent. You can allow Google’s AI anytime in Profile, or the next time you start a reflection." (`copy.journal.notes.permission`). The person is asked again the next time they open New Entry, and never between.
- **Taking it back.** Profile's first privacy row, Share with Google’s AI, is a switch whose subtitle says what on and off mean. A request already on its way finishes. A reflection still waiting on the phone stays there with "Saved · Waiting for your permission in Profile" and the hourglass, never a spinner; the offline banner counts none while the answer is no, because nothing will go by itself. Such a card has no button of its own: it goes when the answer is yes again, or at sign-out, whose dialog counts it.
- **Calm.** The sheet's one motion is its slide, which Reduce Motion removes; it has no haptic, no event and no log line, and nothing on it is crisis red. Only Profile's switch ticks (`selection`, like Share Usage Data). A press closes the sheet once and then acts: a second press while it leaves is ignored. The Privacy Policy link, under Learn more, opens `PRIVACY_POLICY_URL` (the page at gracehush.com) in the in-app browser.
- **The layout.** The sheet is as tall as its text, at most the window less the top inset. The text scrolls inside it, and the caption, both buttons and the link are pinned under the text with 24 plus the bottom inset of padding below them, so Allow and Not now are on screen at every text size and opening Learn more only makes the text scroll. Its height has not been measured on a phone.
- **Scholar (step 38).** The same answer stands in front of Scholar, with a second gate: `scholarRepository.ts` sends no message (`runTurn`) and asks for no answer read aloud (`speak`) unless `hasAiPermission()` says yes, before the mock data and before the network, and `ConversationScreen` is covered by the same sheet until the account has answered ("Not now" goes back; Allow sends a starter, if there is one). The words name Scholar (`AI_WORDING` 2 in the profile prefs store): an account that allowed under the first words sees once, in its first conversation, "Scholar uses the same Google AI as your devotionals. You can change this in Profile." (`useAiWordingNote`; seeing it records the current words, and allowing now records them too). The new words are drafts for the owner and the lawyer.
- **The voice permission (step 39).** A live talk needs a second answer, given before the first talk, and since 2026-11-19 so does Listen in the chosen voice: the ElevenLabs permission ("Allow ElevenLabs to hear and speak for Scholar?"; since 2026-11-19 the body says "ElevenLabs speaks Scholar’s answers in the voice you choose: in a live talk, where it also turns your voice into text, and when you tap Listen. Google’s AI still writes the answers, as it does when you type.", and the trust line that ElevenLabs keeps no recording of the voice, has its training use switched off, and holds a talk's words and a short summary, and each answer it reads aloud, until GraceHush deletes them there soon after; "Nothing is sent until you tap Allow."). It is one component, `VoicePermissionStep`: the Talk sheet's step 1 and, since 2026-11-19, the first step of the chat's voice sheet for an account that has not allowed ElevenLabs. It is `voicePermissionAt` in the profile prefs store, per account and on this phone only, like the AI permission; `hasVoicePermission()` (`services/aiPermission.ts`) is the gate `startTalk` asks first after the AI permission, before the mock data and the network, so nothing reaches ElevenLabs or `scholar-voice-start` without both. Since 2026-11-19 it also decides Listen's voice: the player asks for the chosen voice only with it (`listenVoice()` in `scholarRepository.ts`), and `speak` drops a voice it is handed without it, whoever asks, so a reading reaches ElevenLabs only with both answers too. Profile's Share Your Voice with ElevenLabs switch takes it back; the next Talk asks again, and Listen goes back to the devotional's voice. Ending a talk asks no permission, so a talk whose permission was taken back during it still closes. Not now, the scrim and the back key send nothing. The words are drafts for the owner and the lawyer.
- **Checks:** `supabase/tests/ai-permission/` (below, under Verifying on this machine): the engine's gate, the saved answer, the words, and the source of New Entry and of the sheet. How the sheet behaves (the slide, the guard against a second press, the footer pinned under the scrolling text, VoiceOver kept on the sheet) was proved with a render check of the real component in a scratch harness, with 21 deliberate breaks of the sheet; that harness is not in the repo, so the suite's source checks are what keep the wiring in the repo. The other suites that run the real engine answer yes through a stand-in, so a suite that adds a reflection needs nothing new.

## Analytics Rules (step 34)

Built in step 34 (2026-10-31); the code is `src/services/analytics.ts` (the client) and `src/services/analyticsPolicy.ts` (the catalog and the filter, pure), and the checks are `supabase/tests/analytics/`.

- **Consent is the only gate, and unset is no.** The device's answer is `analyticsConsent` in the onboarding store (`unset`, `granted` or `declined`, since version 3), given on the Journal's consent card on the first visit after sign-in (round 8 of 2026-11-01; onboarding's fourth step from step 34 until then) or with Profile's Share Usage Data switch; the card's Share usage data is the filled button and Not now a plain text button beside it. It belongs to the device, like the reminder times: sign-out keeps it. Only `granted` sends anything, and only when `checkAnalyticsEnv` says ok (both PostHog variables are valid; with neither set, analytics is off and the consent card and the switch are not shown).
- **No client before consent.** The SDK fetches its remote config from PostHog as soon as a client is made, opted out or not, so `new PostHog(...)` runs only once the answer is yes, and once. A person who never says yes never reaches PostHog. The client is made with `defaultOptIn: false` and then `optIn()`, `customStorage: AsyncStorage`, `personProfiles: "never"`, `disableGeoip`, `before_send: sanitizeEvent`, the lifecycle events on, and session replay, feature flags, surveys, error tracking, default person properties and push-notification capture off.
- **The catalog is the only source of events.** Twenty events since step 38 (eighteen before), typed in `analyticsPolicy.ts`, the only names `track()` accepts: `onboarding_completed` (sent from the Journal's consent card since round 8 of 2026-11-01), `reflection_ready`, `entry_failed`, `devotional_played`, `devotional_completed`, `ambient_changed`, `affirmation_favorited`, `affirmation_added`, `affirmation_notification_opened`, `prayer_tracked_changed`, `prayer_marked_answered`, `prayer_removed`, `reflection_deleted`, `paywall_viewed`, `purchase_completed`, `purchase_failed`, `restore_completed` and `data_export_requested`, each with its own few properties (booleans and values from fixed lists; since 2026-11-09 `reflection_ready` carries `input` and `scripture_verified` only, because the audio is made behind the server's answer and `has_audio` would always have read false; `affirmation_added` (2026-11-09) carries `position`, 4 to 10, the place of the one added, and never the entry or a word). The SDK's own events that may leave the phone are `$screen`, `Application Opened`, `Application Became Active` and `Application Backgrounded`. An event is sent after the thing it reports has happened (a heart after its save succeeded, a deletion after the server deleted). A devotional played while sharing was off is not remembered as the last one played: when sharing turns on, the first play is sent (found in the walk-through, B4). Since 2026-11-20 `acquisition_source_answered { source }` (one of `ACQUISITION_SOURCES`: the nine places of onboarding's source step, or `skipped`) is sent once, the first time the device has both an answer and sharing on: at the answer when sharing is already on, or at the yes when the answer was given before it (sharing off, or not asked yet, which is the usual case, because the Journal's card asks after sign-in). The onboarding store's `acquisitionSent` keeps it from being sent again (a changed answer moves only the super property), and the developer reset clears both. It carries one word, never free text.
- **The filter.** Every event passes `sanitizeEvent` (PostHog's `before_send`) on its way to the queue: an event that is not in the catalog or the system list is dropped, and so is one whose own properties are missing or not valid; every other property goes unless the common list keeps it (the SDK's library, OS, device type and session id, `$screen_name` when it is a tracked screen, and the app's `tier`, `app_version`, `build` and, since 2026-11-20, `acquisition_source`); `$set` and `$set_once` go; and `$process_person_profile: false` and `$geoip_disable: true` are forced on every event, whatever came in.
- **Screens.** `trackCurrentScreen()` runs on the NavigationContainer's ready and state changes and sends `$screen` with the route's name only, never its params, for Journal, Scholar, Prayers, Profile, NewEntry, NowPlaying and Paywall (Scholar since step 38). A Scholar conversation (`Conversation`) is never one, and Reflection is never one: the same route shows a heavy moment, and the navigator cannot tell them apart. Onboarding and Sign In are not either (Support Resources was removed on 2026-11-08).
- **Super properties:** `tier` (the entitlement store's plan, when known; unregistered when it becomes unknown), `app_version` and `build` (`development` or `release`), and since 2026-11-20 `acquisition_source` (the device's answer to onboarding's source step, `skipped` included; unregistered while there is none), registered after every opt-in, when the plan changes and when the answer changes, and always before `acquisition_source_answered`, so that event carries it too.
- **Off means off.** Turning sharing off calls `optOut()` and empties the SDK's queue, so nothing captured before leaves the phone either; the client stays in memory, opted out, and after a restart with sharing off none is made.
- **Sign-out and account deletion** end with `resetAnalytics()` (the last line of `signOutCleanup()`): `reset()` gives the next account on the phone a new anonymous id, and the device's consent is applied again (reset also clears the opt-in and the super properties). What was already waiting is sent under the old id.
- **Never** `identify`, `alias`, `group`, person properties, feature flags, surveys, session replay, error tracking, logs (`captureLog`), `debug()`, or an analytics log line in the app.
- **No event about a heavy moment:** no name, property or value is about one; `reflection_ready`, `reflection_deleted`, `prayer_tracked_changed`, `prayer_marked_answered`, `prayer_removed` and `affirmation_added` skip one (2026-11-08: a heavy moment is a whole reflection and may be a prayer), and `paywall_viewed` is never sent for the heavy-moment redirect, which draws nothing of the paywall.
- **Live talks (step 39):** `scholar_talk_started { voice }` once a talk is live, and `scholar_talk_ended { length: under_1 | 1_to_5 | over_5, ended_by: person | cap | left_app | dropped }` when a talk that went live ends; neither is sent about a talk in a heavy conversation, nor when a talk becomes heavy, nor for a talk that never went live. Twenty-two events in all (twenty-three since 2026-11-20, with `acquisition_source_answered`). `ScholarTalk` is not a tracked screen.
- **Scholar (step 38):** `scholar_conversation_started { source: tab | starter | reflection }` with a conversation's first answer and `scholar_message_sent { mode: text | voice; turn: first | follow_up }` with each answer, never a word, an id or a count, and nothing at all about a heavy conversation; `paywall_viewed` may say `source: scholar`.
- **The developer panel** ("Analytics (dev)", `__DEV__` only) shows the env check, the consent, the client, the queue's length, the id's last 8 characters and the last events by name and property keys only, never a value.
