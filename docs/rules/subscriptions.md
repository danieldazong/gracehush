## Subscription Rules (RevenueCat)

- RevenueCat is the source of truth for entitlements. Use one entitlement: `pro`.
- Read offerings and prices at runtime. Show trial terms exactly as configured.
- The Paywall must include Restore Purchases, the trial and renewal terms, Terms of Use, and Privacy Policy (App Store and Play requirements).
- `subscription_tier` is set only on the server, never from the client: since step 30 by `syncSubscription` (`_shared/billing/revenuecat.ts`), which re-reads RevenueCat for the webhook, for `sync-subscription` and for process-entry's expiry guard (Billing, under Backend / Edge Function Rules).
- Gate Pro features in the UI with `useEntitlement()` (`src/hooks/useEntitlement.ts`, step 30: what RevenueCat last reported for the signed-in account), but always enforce limits on the server too.
- **What Pro is in V1 (decided in the step 16 review, 2026-10-21; Scholar's part revised 2026-11-18):** unlimited audio devotionals, and Scholar: its messages, its answers read aloud and the live talk. **Since 2026-11-18 Scholar is part of a paying Pro plan alone, and the free trial does not include it** (the owner: "Dont includes Scholar for Free trial"): a trial gets the unlimited devotionals, and Scholar opens when its plan begins. A Pro account spends credits from an allowance of 400 that renews 24 hours after its first spend (`SCHOLAR_CREDITS` in `_shared/scholar/constants.ts`): a message costs 4, and an answer read aloud 3 for every 100 characters of its whole script, at least 3, paid on its first part. The live talk spends no credits and has its own time: 60 minutes a calendar month in the profile's time zone (30 until 2026-11-18) and at most 10 minutes a talk (a talk's own cap is the smaller of 10 minutes and what is left of the month, and under 30 seconds left counts as used up; a talk has at most 80 turns and an account 20 starts a day: `_shared/voice/constants.ts`). A message from an account that is not Pro is kept, after a clear screening, with Try again, which answers it once the plan includes Scholar: a free account is offered the plans, and a trial is told the day its plan begins, with no paywall. Every paywall's four rows are "Unlimited audio devotionals", "Scholar, your Bible companion", "Scholar reads its answers aloud" and "Talk with Scholar out loud", and while the selected plan has a free trial a line under the plans says what it covers. The numbers live on the server, reach the app in each answer's `usage` and may change with a deploy; the app holds no limit number. A heavy conversation is never limited, counted or paywalled, for any plan, and anyone may listen there; a Pro account's talk in one is never counted or cut, and an account that is not Pro has no talk there (it sees no Talk button, so no paywall or plan notice appears in a hard moment). Until 2026-11-18 Pro gave longer conversations with Scholar (40 messages a day against the free plan's 5), 5 answers read aloud a day and 30 minutes of talk a month, the trial had Pro's Scholar, and a ceiling on free messages across the whole service (`SCHOLAR_FREE_DAILY_BUDGET`) answered "Scholar is resting" above it. Free users get `FREE_DEVOTIONALS_PER_WEEK` a week (Backend rule 10). The Paywall and Profile's Pro card may advertise only a benefit that is a real gate in this file. The design's other four rows (ambient soundscapes, a "never expires" history, the answered prayer timeline and a choice of devotional voices) are not gates today: the first and third are free for everyone, the history has no retention rule, and the voice is one server-side setting. To advertise one, first make it a gate here, then add its row. **The free conversation (2026-11-20, the co-founder's plan, the owner's "Go ahead and Implement").** Every account without Pro, a trial included, has one free conversation with Scholar: the first conversation Scholar answers for it (a clear message or a talk claims it; a heavy message never does), held for good in `scholar_free_use` (migration 16), so deleting it gives nothing back. In it up to `SCHOLAR_FREE.messages` (8) messages are answered, Listen works on its answers, and live talks run up to `SCHOLAR_FREE.talkSeconds` (180) in all, counted across its talks (under 30 seconds left counts as used up); nothing there spends credits. A free account hears only the default voice (Hannah, `warm`): the server reads and speaks in `warm` whatever the phone asks, and the other seven voices show a PRO chip that opens the Paywall (`reason: "voice"`; Preview stays free); a trial keeps all eight. Beyond it (a ninth message there, or any message in another conversation) a free account's message is kept after its screening and the Paywall opens by itself with "You’ve had your free conversation with Scholar. Scholar is part of GraceHush Pro." (`reason: "conversation"`), and a trial's is kept with the day its plan begins and no paywall. From three free messages left the conversation says how many remain (`SCHOLAR_LEFT_NOTICE_FROM`). A heavy message is answered on every plan and never counted toward the 8 or the 180.
- Offer a free trial only when the package has one **and** the store confirms this user is eligible; never advertise a trial that cannot be confirmed, and never show a savings percentage that the prices do not support.
- The Paywall never says "cancel with one tap", "no commitments" or that a failed purchase "has not been charged": cancellation happens in the App Store or Google Play settings, and a dropped connection can follow a charge. The disclosure names where, and a failed purchase points to Restore purchases.
- **Purchases run on RevenueCat (step 30)**: `src/services/purchases.ts` (the same four calls and return shapes as step 16) over `src/services/revenuecat.ts`, the only file that imports the SDK. The mock data source keeps the step 16 mock and its five dev flags, and a release build never reaches it (its data source is always the real one).
- **The key** is chosen at run time (`chooseRevenueCatKey`, `src/constants/env.ts`): Expo Go takes only the Test Store key (`test_`; RevenueCat's Browser Mode refuses anything else); a native build takes its platform's key (`appl_`, `goog_`) and only in development falls back to the Test Store key, because RevenueCat crashes a release build that uses one; a secret key (`sk_`) is never used, whatever else is set. Each key is read by its full name.
- **Identity**: RevenueCat is configured only after sign-in, with the Clerk user id (`logIn` on an account switch, `logOut` at sign-out with errors ignored), so no purchase is ever made as an anonymous customer. RevenueCat's own `logOut` leaves an empty anonymous customer behind, and the next account's `logIn` renames it to that account: its `original_app_user_id` stays the anonymous id, kept as an alias, which the webhook skips (seen in the step 30 walk-through; it carries nothing from the account before). Purchases run only while RevenueCat is logged in as the signed-in account: a login that failed never sends a purchase to the previous account, and the next refresh (each foreground) logs in again.
- **The plan the screens show** is what RevenueCat last reported for the signed-in account (the entitlement store), otherwise the server's plan from the profile sync, otherwise free (`getUsage`). The sync engine and the reflections the free limit held follow the server's plan only (`getKnownTier`), because only the server decides a 402.
- **Each foreground** reads the entitlement again (Expo Go sends no customer-info updates) and, when RevenueCat's plan and the server's differ, asks the server to look once (`startPlanWatch`, `src/services/subscriptionSync.ts`); at sign-in the same check runs once the first entitlement and the first profile sync have both answered. A native build also listens for RevenueCat's updates.
- **Trials** are advertised only when confirmed: on iOS `checkTrialOrIntroductoryPriceEligibility` answers `ELIGIBLE`, on Android the package's default option has a free phase (Google Play sends it only to someone eligible); Expo Go never confirms one, and the Test Store products have no trial.
- **Purchase errors** map to the step 16 codes: a cancel (`userCancelled`, or `PURCHASE_CANCELLED_ERROR`) is `CANCELLED`, `PAYMENT_PENDING_ERROR` is `PENDING`, `NETWORK_ERROR` and `OFFLINE_CONNECTION_ERROR` are `NETWORK`, `PURCHASE_NOT_ALLOWED_ERROR` is `NOT_ALLOWED`, `PRODUCT_ALREADY_PURCHASED_ERROR` is `ALREADY_OWNED` (the Paywall restores), anything else (the Test Store's failed purchase included) is `UNKNOWN`. A purchase that gives no `pro` (its product is not attached to the entitlement) is `UNKNOWN`.
- **Logs**: the SDK logs at WARN in every build (its DEBUG and INFO lines print the app user id); the app never logs CustomerInfo, a receipt, a key, a user id or a price, and its one development line is `[billing] sync <ok or code>`.
- **Sandbox**: the server counts sandbox purchases like real ones (the Test Store and TestFlight need that), so before launch RevenueCat's Sandbox Testing Access is limited to the test accounts (Before a store build).
- **Launch pricing (revised twice on 2026-11-18; $9.99 a month and $59.99 a year were adopted on 2026-10-22):** $125.99 a year, the plan that is selected first, with a 3-day free trial on the yearly plan only (the trial includes the unlimited devotionals and not Scholar, which opens when the plan begins), and $14.99 a month with no trial. That is the 30% saving the Paywall computes from the fetched prices (12 × $14.99 is $179.88). A product manager's plan proposed them, and the owner approved it with "Go ahead and Implement payment and all 3 recommendations"; earlier the same day the launch prices were $69.99 a year with a 7-day trial and $12.99 a month (approved with "Implement your Recommendation (one plan)"). These are the products to create in App Store Connect and Play Console (step 35, and an Android build) and in RevenueCat: the user sets the prices there, they are never typed into the app, and `data/mock/offerings.ts` holds the launch prices as placeholders for the development preview (so the mock Paywall and the reminder's developer test show $125.99 with its 3-day trial and $14.99). RevenueCat's Test Store products (made in step 30: `gracehush_pro_yearly` at 59.99 USD and `gracehush_pro_monthly` at 9.99 USD, no trial, because Expo Go cannot confirm one, in the `pro` entitlement and the current `default` offering) keep their test prices, since a product's price cannot be edited after it is saved, so the Paywall on the real RevenueCat path in a development build shows $59.99 and $9.99 until two new Test Store products are made at the new prices (the owner's, in the RevenueCat dashboard, with its yes first: products are RevenueCat's; the Test Store has no trial); production uses the new price points. **The rest of the recommendation:** list the app in the US storefront only at launch (its prices, its tax forms and its legal pages are all US); enroll in Apple's App Store Small Business Program before the first sale (15% instead of 30% while the developer's proceeds stay under $1 million a year); launch value-first as built (3 free devotionals a week, then the Paywall) and do not copy a hard paywall yet; the trial-end reminder is built (Notification Rules); Scholar is for paying Pro, with its credits (What Pro is, above). **Recorded and not built, each needing the owner's yes:** a hidden fair-use cap of 3 devotionals a day on Pro (today only the 10-in-10-minutes rate limit stops a daily listener, about 1,400 a day: a cap conflicts with the Paywall's "Unlimited audio devotionals" row and needs a fair-use sentence in the Terms, a wording decision and a server change); a hard-paywall variant and a paid 30-day trial of about $2.99, to be tested against the current flow with RevenueCat Experiments once there is traffic and before any installs are paid for. **The model behind it (2026-11-18; assumptions, not GraceHush data, except the API costs below):** after Apple's 15% a yearly member leaves $8.92 a month and a monthly one $12.74; a member's use is about $4.30 a month (20 reflections about $1.10; 60 Scholar messages and 8 answers read aloud about $0.45; 30 minutes of talk about $2.58, at about $0.086 a minute; other costs about $0.15), and the free users cost about $2.00 a month for each paying member, so a monthly member leaves about $8.44 a month ($6.44 after the free users) and a yearly one about $4.62 ($2.62); the content tools (Arcads $110 and Higgsfield $49) and the rest of the fixed costs come to about $235 a month, covered by about 37 monthly or 90 yearly members, about 60 at the expected mix. The credits cap what one person's Scholar can cost: a credit is about $0.001, so 400 a day is at most about $0.40 a day, up to about $12 a month, and the talk at most about $5.16 a month (60 minutes); a member who spends every credit every day and the whole month's talk costs about $18 a month, more than either plan leaves, and the averages above assume most do not. Paid installs lose money until the cost per paying subscriber is measured: organic posting and the tools only until it is under what a member leaves. The least certain input is the live talk (one 10-minute test talk cost 4,400 ElevenLabs credits and the balance was not read). Re-run the numbers with real usage after the first 50 or so subscribers. Changing a price later is a store and RevenueCat edit, not a release. Test a longer trial with RevenueCat Experiments once there is traffic (a longer trial may convert better, and it also raises forgot-to-cancel refunds). Before the yearly price is locked, check what one devotional costs in Gemini and Cloud Text-to-Speech with the real voice, because the yearly plan caps what a daily listener pays. Measured on 2026-10-24 with the real voice (Aoede at 0.88): Chirp 3 HD is $30 per million characters after the first million each month, and a real reflection's script is 1,688 characters (the passage included), so about $0.051 of speech, plus about $0.0017 of Gemini for the screening and the reflection: about $0.052 a devotional, and the first million characters a month cover about 590 of them. A daily listener therefore costs about $19 a year to serve, against the yearly price before the stores' share ($59.99 when this was measured, $125.99 now). The estimate before the real run was $16 to $19 (1,300 to 1,700 characters); one reflection is one sample. Step 32 shortened the devotional to about a minute and a half: its tone evaluation's 24 scripts (`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, 2026-10-30) had a median of 1,327 characters (1,146 to 1,627), about $0.040 of speech, plus about $0.0026 of Gemini for the screening and the reflection, so about $0.042 a devotional; the free million characters a month cover about 750 of them, and a daily listener costs about $15 a year.
