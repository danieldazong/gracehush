# Scholar and hard moments

## If you are in danger right now

Don’t wait for an answer from Scholar.

- In the US, call or text 988 (the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), any time, day or night. You can also chat at 988lifeline.org.
- If someone is hurting you, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.
- If your life is in danger, call 911.
- Outside the US, call your local emergency number. You can find a help line in your country at findahelpline.com.

## What Scholar is

Scholar is GraceHush’s AI Bible companion. You can ask it about a verse, bring it a feeling or a hard day, and ask follow-up questions.

Scholar is a computer program. It is not a person, a pastor or a counselor, and it does not replace your church, your doctor or a counselor. GraceHush is not a crisis service. Scholar reminds you that it is an AI when a conversation starts, and again every three hours while you keep talking.

## How Scholar answers

Scholar’s answers are written by Google’s AI (Gemini), following GraceHush’s own rules. To write an answer, your message is sent to Google, and only after you say yes in the app. Google’s paid terms, which GraceHush uses, say Google does not use what you send to improve its products; it may keep it for a limited time to check for misuse. GraceHush does not use your conversations to train AI models.

Bible passages are shown in passage cards, read word for word from the Berean Standard Bible. Scholar can be wrong. If something it says troubles you, talk it over with someone you trust.

## When a message shows you may be in danger

Every message is checked before any part of its answer is shown, read aloud or saved. The check uses GraceHush’s own list of words and Google’s AI. It looks for signs that you may be thinking about ending your life or harming yourself, that someone is harming you, that you may harm someone else, or that you are in danger right now.

When it finds them, Scholar still answers you, with extra care:

- It stays with what you are going through. It does not repeat back what you wrote about harm, so the answer never dwells on it.
- It never asks how, when or where, and it never gives you a list of questions.
- It speaks of God’s nearness and encourages you to reach a real person today.
- It never ends the conversation or refuses to talk with you.
- If that answer cannot be written in time, you get a short answer GraceHush wrote ahead of time. You are never asked to try again.
- If your message says harm is already happening, GraceHush shows its own short answer at once, with the emergency number first.

Under that answer the app shows a help line that fits your situation, with buttons to call or text. If you listen to the answer, the help line is read aloud after it.

For the rest of that conversation, every answer is written with the same care, and a small help button stays within reach. A new conversation starts fresh. There is one exception: a conversation you open from a reflection that was itself written with extra care keeps that care.

A message like this never counts toward your daily limit and never brings up a paywall. In that conversation you can listen to answers on any plan.

## What happens, and what does not

- Nobody is contacted. GraceHush does not contact emergency services, your family, your church or anyone else. The call and text buttons only open your own phone’s dialer or messages.
- No one at GraceHush reads your conversations as they happen. A few people who run the service can technically reach our database; they open a conversation only to fix a problem you report to us, and only with your permission, or when the law requires it.
- The conversation is marked so that its later answers follow the same care, and the help line you were shown is saved as part of the conversation, as one word, so the same line shows when you open it again. Both are deleted when you delete the conversation, the reflection it came from, or your account. No category, reason or phrase is stored, and nothing marks your account.
- No analytics event is sent about that message or about the rest of that conversation.
- GraceHush keeps one anonymous count a day of how many messages were checked and how many needed extra care. Nothing in it points to any person.
- If you speak a message, Google’s AI (Gemini) turns the recording into text, and the recording is then deleted.
- Your conversations stay in your account until you delete them. Anyone who can open your phone can read them. You can delete a conversation in Scholar at any time, and deleting your account deletes them all.

## The check can miss

The check is automatic. It can miss a message from someone in danger, and it can flag a message from someone who is not. If you are in danger, don’t wait for an answer from Scholar. Use the numbers at the top of this page.

## Who Scholar is for

GraceHush is made for adults, 18 and over. If you tell Scholar you are younger, in a hard moment it still answers you with the same care and points you to the help line and to an adult you trust. Otherwise it tells you kindly that GraceHush is for adults, and Scholar then closes for your account. If that was a mistake, write to us at [{{SUPPORT_EMAIL}}](mailto:{{SUPPORT_EMAIL}}).
